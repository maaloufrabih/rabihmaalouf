About

Jurist, strategist, and trusted adviser to international institutions

Strategic Adviser where law, policy, and power converge.

Rabih Maalouf advises on how power actually operates through law and policy - how rules get made and by whom, where enforcement really sits, which institutions can be moved and which cannot, and what a decision will cost once interests start pushing back against it. The reading comes from having watched the same dynamics from opposite sides. Twenty years as a judge in Lebanon, deciding complex corporate and criminal cases, taught him how a law operates when it meets a party with the means to resist it. Work with government entities on policy matters, together with appearances before legislative bodies governing those cases, exposed the other side of the equation: how rules are conceived, negotiated, and made. Between the two lies a career in institutions: advising the International Monetary Fund (IMF), United Nations Office of Drugs and Crime (UNODC), and the Geneva Center for Security Sector Governance (DCAF) on various issues such as financial integrity, international cooperation and governance of security agencies, to name a few; advising the Alternative Disputes Resolution Department at the Abu Dhabi Judicial Department (ADJD); and counseling the Special Tribunal for Lebanon (STL) on criminal law matters. Across these roles, he has moved between deciding, drafting, advising, and building institutions, each perspective sharpening the others.

Artificial intelligence is the latest expression of the same question. As an AI Fellow at the Ford Foundation and a lecturer in Constitutional Law and AI Policy at the American University of Beirut, he works on how AI should be governed. The technology may be new, the institutional problem is not: how a rule is written, how it is applied, and what happens when it meets the realities of power. Engagements are limited in number and handled confidentially.
